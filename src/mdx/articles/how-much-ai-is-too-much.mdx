AI tools are great. I use them almost every day.
They help me write boilerplate code, explain errors, fix syntax issues, and speed up parts of my work, But if you're just getting started, you need to be careful.

AI can make you faster.  
But it can also stop you from learning.

## What AI is good at

AI excels at several specific tasks that can boost your productivity:

- **Explaining errors in simple terms** - Breaking down complex error messages
- **Showing you common patterns and examples** - Providing code templates and best practices
- **Saving time on repetitive code** - Generating boilerplate and repetitive structures
- **Giving you quick summaries** - Condensing documentation and library information

That's all helpful, especially when you're stuck or working on something new.

## What AI can't do for you

- Teach you how things work, step by step
- Help you understand why something is the right approach
- Make design decisions
- Help you when the output is wrong but looks right

When you're early in your career, you need to do the hard parts.  
You need to think through the logic, try different paths, and fix your own mistakes.

That's how you build real skill.

## What I've learned

I've used AI to help with small things :- naming functions, debugging, generating test cases.

But I never copy big chunks of code I don't understand.

If I do use AI, I make sure I can explain every line it gave me.

If I can't, I throw it out and try again myself.

## Use it like a second brain, not a shortcut

AI is like a smart assistant. It's there to help, but you should be in control.

Use it when:

- You've tried something yourself first
- You want to double-check your thinking
- You're trying to save time, not skip learning

Don't use it to avoid the hard parts. That's where the learning is.

## If you're a fresher, here's my take

- Use AI when you're stuck, but try on your own first
- Read the output, don't just copy it
- Ask AI for explanations, not full answers
- Try to break the problem down before you ask for help

You'll get better faster that way.

AI is useful. No question.  
But if you're new, focus on learning how to think.  
Not just how to prompt.

Use AI. But still do the work.
