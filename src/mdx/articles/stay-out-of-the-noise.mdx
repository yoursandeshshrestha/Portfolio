Every team has a bit of noise.

There will be disagreements, small fights, side comments, and sometimes politics. It shows up even in companies that are doing well.

You can’t avoid it completely, but you can choose not to be part of it.

When I started working, I thought I needed to be in every conversation. I thought that having an opinion on everything meant I was being helpful. But over time, I noticed something.

The people who made the most impact were not the ones who talked the most. They were the ones who stayed focused. They showed up, did the work, helped others, and quietly moved things forward.

> "The loudest person in the room is not always the most useful one."

## What does noise look like?

It’s when people argue about things that do not affect the product.  
It’s when people talk about each other instead of the problem.  
It’s complaining without offering help.  
It’s sharing opinions just to be seen.  
It’s pulling others into drama that leads nowhere.

It might feel important in the moment, but most of it adds no real value. It just slows things down.

## What I do now

What I do now is simple.

If something is useful, I speak up.  
If I don’t understand something, I ask.  
If I can help, I offer.

But I stay away from anything that burns time and energy for no reason.

I don’t join side chats where nothing gets built. I don’t pick sides in fights that have nothing to do with the work. I don’t bring emotion into every problem.

> "Focus is not just what you work on, it’s also what you choose to ignore."

## Focus on the work

The more time I put into building, shipping, and helping my team, the better things go. Not just in terms of the product, but in how people treat me.

When you're consistent, clear, and useful, people notice. Even if you don’t speak much. Even if you don’t try to manage anyone.

> "Good work cuts through noise."

## That does not mean stay silent

Staying out of the noise doesn’t mean staying silent. If something is wrong, or unfair, or broken — speak up. You should not let things slide just to avoid conflict.

But not every disagreement deserves your attention. Learn to pick your battles. Let the rest go.

## What has worked for me

- I write more than I talk
- I ask clear questions and skip gossip
- I help the people who help others
- I say less when emotions are high
- I keep track of things when needed, but I don’t carry grudges

> "You don’t need to play games to grow. Just be useful, stay honest, and stay out of the noise."

That’s what I try to do.
